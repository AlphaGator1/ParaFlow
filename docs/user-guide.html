ParaFlow

User guide

Hold a key, say what you want written, and let go.

ParaFlow types your words wherever your cursor already is — an email, a chat box, a code editor, a search field. There is no window to switch to and nothing to paste. Everything runs on this computer, and your voice never leaves it.

Getting started

Click into whatever you want to write in, then:

  1. Hold down Ctrl + Win. That is the starting combination. You can change it — see Changing the key.
  2. Wait for the short rising chime. That is your microphone opening. The very first press of a session takes a moment; every press after that is instant, because the microphone stays open between sentences.
  3. Speak normally. Do not slow down or over-enunciate. Ordinary conversational speech is what it is best at.
  4. Let go of the key.
  5. Your words are typed in. Usually about a tenth of a second after you release.

For anything longer than a sentence or two, don't hold the key — tap it twice quickly and ParaFlow keeps listening with nothing held down. See Dictating more than a sentence.

Leave the cursor where you want the text. ParaFlow types into whichever window is in front at the moment it finishes. If you click into something else while it is still working, that is where the words will land.

ParaFlow has no main window. It lives in the system tray, next to the clock — click the icon there for settings and everything else. If you cannot see it, click the small ^ arrow to show hidden icons.

It also does not start with Windows. Launch it from the Start menu when you want it, and quit it from the tray when you are done.

Give it a few seconds after starting. ParaFlow loads two models onto your graphics card when it launches. Until that finishes, the push-to-talk key does nothing at all — no chime, no overlay change. If your very first press seems to be ignored, wait a moment and press again.

The small panel that appears near the edge of your screen is ParaFlow telling you what it is doing. It is worth learning to read, because it is also how you know the microphone state.

The LIVE badge

On the right of the panel is a badge reading either LIVE or OFF. This is the plain answer to "is my microphone on right now?", and it is deliberately the most prominent thing on the overlay after your text. Hover over it to see which microphone is in use.

Moving it, or hiding it

Click the ParaFlow icon in the system tray and choose Overlay position — Bottom, Top or Centre. Settings adds a fourth option, Hidden.

You cannot drag the overlay, and that is on purpose: it is click-through, so your clicks pass straight to the window behind it and it can never catch the text you are dictating. A panel you could grab would be a panel that could steal a click at the wrong moment.

When the microphone is actually on

A dictation app that quietly listens all day is not the deal. Here is exactly what ParaFlow does.

  • It starts closed. Launching ParaFlow does not open your microphone. Pressing the key does.
  • A rising two-note chime means it is live. A falling two-note chime means it has closed. These are quiet, but deliberately not silent — they are the audible half of the promise.
  • It stays open between sentences. Reopening the device on every press would put a delay in front of every sentence, so after your first press it is held open, ready.
  • It closes itself after 30 seconds of no dictation, and you hear the falling chime when it does. Change that timeout in Settings — anything from 5 seconds to an hour.
  • Hands-free recording is the one case where it stays open while you are not pressing anything, and it is also the one case with a hard two-minute ceiling. See the next section.
  • Very short taps are thrown away. Anything held for less than a quarter of a second is discarded rather than transcribed, so brushing the key does not produce stray text.

Nothing is recorded to disk. Your audio exists in memory for as long as it takes to transcribe the sentence you just said, and is then gone. There is no recordings folder, because there are no recordings.

To close the microphone immediately rather than waiting out the timer, quit ParaFlow from the tray menu. Restarting it is quick, and it starts closed again.

Dictating more than a sentence

Holding a key down for two minutes is no way to write a paragraph. Tap your combination twice quickly and ParaFlow keeps listening with nothing held down.

  1. Tap the combination twice, quickly. Like a double-click. If it does not take, you were a little slow — try again slightly faster.
  2. Let go and talk. The overlay changes to Hands-free with a clock counting up, and its edge glows so it cannot be mistaken for idle.
  3. Press the combination once to finish. A single press. Everything you said is transcribed and typed in one go.

It stops itself after two minutes. The overlay counts down for the last twenty seconds so it is never a surprise, and everything recorded up to that point is transcribed and typed exactly as if you had pressed the key yourself. Nothing is thrown away. Double-tap again to carry on.

That limit is deliberate. It is roughly how much audio ParaFlow holds in memory, and it means a microphone left recording because somebody walked away turns itself off.

Because cleanup sees the whole recording at once, dictating a long passage this way tends to come out better than the same words in short bursts — it can punctuate across sentences and lay out a dictated list properly.

If you find yourself latching by accident, turn it off: Settings → Push-to-talk key → Double-tap to keep recording. With it off, the combination only ever works press-and-hold.

What cleanup does

Speech and writing are not the same thing. If you installed ParaFlow with cleanup, a second pass turns one into the other before anything is typed.

- Faster startup

Correcting yourself out loud

This is the feature most worth knowing about. Say "Tuesday, no, Wednesday" and only Wednesday is typed — Tuesday does not appear at all. Words like no, sorry, I mean, actually and rather mark a correction, and the later version always wins.

Spoken formatting

These are treated as instructions rather than words: new line, new paragraph, bullet point, period, comma and question mark.

It will never answer you

Dictate "write a Python function that reverses a string" and you get that sentence typed out — not a function. ParaFlow is a typist, not an assistant. It is built and tested that way on purpose, because dictation that occasionally decides to help would be unusable.

You never lose your words. If cleanup fails, times out, or returns something that does not look like a transcript, ParaFlow types the plain unpolished transcript instead. It will never swallow a sentence.

Cleanup costs roughly a tenth of a second per sentence. If you would rather have the raw transcript, turn it off in Settings and restart ParaFlow. If you installed the dictation-only option, cleanup is not present at all and nothing in this section applies.

It notices where you are typing

A shell command and an email should not come out looking the same. Cleanup checks which application is in front and matches it.

Where you are typingWhat you get
Editors, IDEs and terminals — VS Code, Cursor, Visual Studio, the JetBrains tools, Sublime Text, Notepad++, Vim, Emacs, Zed, Windows Terminal, PowerShell, Command Prompt Commands stay commands: no invented capital letter, no trailing full stop, and "dash" and "slash" become - and /. Ordinary sentences dictated into an editor still get normal punctuation.
Chat — Slack, Discord, Teams, Telegram, WhatsApp, Signal Short and conversational.
Email — Outlook, Thunderbird, Mailspring, eM Client Complete sentences, slightly more formal.
Everything else, including web browsers Ordinary writing.

Browsers are deliberately left as ordinary writing. A browser tab could be a code review or a birthday message, and guessing wrong is worse than not guessing.

Teaching it your words

Colleagues' names, product names, acronyms, library names — the words a general-purpose recogniser has never met. Write them down once and ParaFlow will spell them your way.

  1. Open Settings from the tray icon, and click Edit personal dictionary… The tray menu has the same item if you prefer.
  2. Add one name or term per line, capitalised exactly as you want it typed. Lines beginning with # are notes to yourself and are ignored.
  3. Save the file and restart ParaFlow. The list is read once at startup, so an edit does not take effect until then.

This is more capable than it sounds. ParaFlow does not just match sounds — it reads the whole sentence, so "the kuber netes cluster" becomes "the Kubernetes cluster" once Kubernetes is in your list.

The dictionary is only used when cleanup is switched on. With the dictation-only install there is nothing to apply it.

Changing the push-to-talk key

  1. Settings → Push-to-talk key → Change…
  2. Hold your combination down, then release it. Every key is captured while it is listening, so nothing else on your computer reacts.
  3. Press Save settings.

Choose something you would never hit while typing. Combinations of modifier keys work well — Ctrl+Win, Ctrl+Alt, Ctrl+Shift+Space — because you can hold them comfortably and they do not collide with ordinary writing.

If your combination is not picked up: Windows keeps a handful of Windows-key shortcuts for itself (Win+E, Win+R and friends) and never passes them on. Work around it like this — click Change…, click on any other window so it has focus, then press your combination. ParaFlow's system-wide listener will catch it even though the settings window cannot.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
The overlay showed my text, but nothing was typed. The application you are typing into is almost certainly running as administrator. Windows does not let an ordinary program send keystrokes to an elevated one. Either run that application normally, or run ParaFlow as administrator too.
Nothing happens when I press the key. If ParaFlow has only just started, give it a few seconds — the key does nothing until both models have loaded. Otherwise another program has probably claimed the same combination; pick a different one in Settings.
The text went into the wrong window. ParaFlow types into whatever is in front when it finishes. Stay put until the text appears.
The beginning of my sentence is missing. Wait for the rising chime before you start speaking. ParaFlow keeps a fraction of a second of audio from before you pressed, but not more than that.
It started recording hands-free when I did not mean it to. Two ordinary presses close together read as a double-tap. Press once to stop, or switch the whole thing off in Settings → Double-tap to keep recording.
My double-tap does not latch. Tap a little faster. ParaFlow uses your Windows double-click speed, so if you have set that very short you have a very short window; Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Mouse → Additional mouse settings adjusts it.
I did not hear a chime. Either chimes are switched off in Settings, or the microphone was already open — it only chimes when it opens, not on every press.
The wrong microphone is being used. ParaFlow uses whatever Windows has set as the default input device. Change it in Windows Settings → System → Sound, then wait for ParaFlow's microphone to close — the device is chosen fresh each time it opens.
My new dictionary word is being ignored. Restart ParaFlow. The list is read once at startup.
Windows warned me when I ran the installer. ParaFlow is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC". Choose More info → Run anyway.
The overlay is in my way. Tray icon → Overlay position, or set it to Hidden in Settings. Clicks already pass straight through it.

Your privacy

  • Nothing is uploaded. There is no account, no sign-in and no telemetry. Both speech recognition and cleanup run on your own graphics card.
  • Nothing is recorded. Audio is held in memory for the sentence being transcribed and then discarded.
  • The only time ParaFlow uses the internet is the one-off download during first-run setup, from github.com and huggingface.co. After that you can disconnect the network entirely and dictation still works — worth trying once, if you want to see it for yourself.
  • The microphone is closed unless you opened it, and it tells you both when it opens and when it closes.

Uninstalling

Windows Settings → Apps → Installed apps → ParaFlow → Uninstall. ParaFlow adds nothing to your startup items and makes no system-wide changes, so removing the program is clean.

Your models are not removed, even if you tick "delete application data". That leaves several gigabytes in %LOCALAPPDATA%\ParaFlow. It is convenient if you reinstall later — nothing is downloaded again — but if you are finished with ParaFlow for good, delete that folder by hand afterwards to get the space back.

Paste %LOCALAPPDATA% into the File Explorer address bar to get there, and delete the ParaFlow folder inside it. That also removes your settings and your personal dictionary, so keep a copy of dictionary.txt if you have put work into it.
